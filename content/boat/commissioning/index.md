As we were finishing up outfitting the boat, we were also working through commissioning -- verifying that all the boat's
system were working as intended.  It was a lot to work though, but if I had it to do over, I'd have worked even harder. 
It's much easier to resolve any potential warranty issues at the handover stage rather than later.  For example, it was 
only later we noticed a small leak in the heat exchange pipe for the heater.  Catching all these potential problems involves 
a lot of crawling around the boat, opening access panels and peering with a flash light, etc.

Even when an issue is uncovered, getting it resolved is no easy task.  We couldn't talk to Fountaine Pajot directly, we had to
report issues to our dealer who reported them to FP who then works with their contractors (Gulfstream, Uchimata and Pochan) to fix the problem.
Many boat buyers hire a local agent who is familiar with the different parties, but we didn't go that route, so it was a bit hard.
Getting all the issues sorted out too much longer than we could have imagined.  We were in La Rochelle from Feb 13 to March 27, 2023.
Six weeks from when we first stepped on the boat till we sailed away from La Rochelle (we had expected a week or two orginally).

Beyond the boat issues we also needed to finalize our US Coast Guard documentation, MMSI number, title, insurance, etc. 

In addition to dealing with FP, we also had to work with Pochan (the installers of the Integrel system) to resolve any problems with 
that system.  Sometime there's a fuzzy line of which party is responsible (e.g. MPPT controls for the 48v battery) in which there is
a lot of "it's that other guys problem".

THe problems we found with the boat were not critical, just minor things (though you'd definitely hope to have them resolved before the handover).  This is the list of issues we uncovered:

* Missing cabinet door in master cabin
* counter to left of sink needs to be buffed out
* Gelcoat chip by life raft locker
* Leg on lounge deck railing is not connected to deck
* Compass is broken
* Depth sounder not installed
* scratch on starboard side saloon window
* Stanchion for helm roof is leaking
* Integrel system needs to be reconfigured use solar panels to charge 48v bank and SOC monitor
* We ordered the Miele 7440 Oven with combined microwave but got the Miele 2760 bp electric (no microwave)
* Missing lid for trash receptacle (didn't know this was a thing till I saw our neighbors Tanna)
* Fans installed in heads rather than saloon (this took months to address)
* Missing smoke detector in saloon
* Leak in port rudder
* Leak in the hydraulic steering system
* One of the panels in the master closet is missing two screws


Eventually almost all of these got dealt with.  The leak in the hydraulic steering system was only discovered the day we left La Rochelle and
it got fixed in Falmouth.  Never did get the missing screws for the closet panel!

Our dealer from Seattle flew over and spent a week with us helping with the checkout and dealing with FP.
Beyond the commissioning and outfitting efforts we also had three training sessions with our French coaches: Jean and Thomas.
Not withstanding some language barriers, they walked us through the different boat systems and we took out the boat three times
for practice sails.  Topics covered where: docking, autopilot use, raising/lowering the sails, reefing, anchoring, emergency tiller use --
it was a lot to take in over six hours.  Even though we had plenty of experience in our 40 ft monohull, the Tanna was a different matter.
For example, when docking you could leave the helm alone and just use the port vs starboard engines to manuever (like driving a tank).  Still the size, momentum, and sight lines left you feeling a little like moving a tennis court around the harbour.   This was actually the only times we left the dock - we didn't have an assigned berth, so if we left on our
own there was a chance we wouldn't have a spot to come back to!  So other than these training sessions, we'd have to be practicing these skills
out in the open ocean.

Anyway, it was a busy six weeks, but finally it was time to cast off.  Our plan for the first season was to head north to England and
then travel deep into the Baltic Sea.  Pretty ambitious for two catamaran newbies!  Checkout [Season1](../posts/season1) to learn how we faired.
