We moved the boat a few miles down the coast from Mellieha Bay (where we first landed in Malta) to St. Julian's Bay.  
Initially we were planning to dock in Valletta itself, but there was no space (and no anchoring).  But St. Julian's was just a 
short bus ride to Valletta, so was a good base for exploring Valletta and the island of Malta as well.
  Even though the distance wasn't far, it
took quite some time for the bus to work it's way through the crowded traffic.

After getting off the bus, we walked through Valletta's old town which sits on the peninsula between the two large
harbors.  We happened across a convent where nuns had lived for many years (there are still a few in residence) 
completely cut off from the bustling city around them.

Next we visited the National Museum of Archaeology.  Malta has had a long string of different groups
living in Malta, starting with paleolithic tribes (and continuing to the Phoenicians, Greeks, Romans, Arabs, etc).
The museum had gathered many of the interesting artifacts recovered around Malta.  Rong was most interested in the pre-bronze age
civilization.  That had build many impressive monuments, from 4000 - 2500 BC.  For unknown reasons, this group disapeared and Malta was 
abondened until it was re-populated in the bronze age.

After the museum we toured the fort that stands impressively high above the harbour.  We even waited for the 4:00 firing of the 
signal gun.  Rather than take the bus back to St. Julian's, we walked from the downtown and took the ferry back to the bay.  It was a nice walk 
scarcely taking longer than the morning's bus ride.
