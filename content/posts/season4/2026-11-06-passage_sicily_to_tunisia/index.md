After an evening viewing the eruptions on the North side of Stromboli volcano, we turned west to sail
along the north coast of Sicily.  We had light but steady winds on a broad reach.  We just used the genoa (since I didn't want to 
be bothered with reefing at night and with a broad reach, the main doesn't add much to our speed anyway)
and made good progress till the next morning.  Then the wind died so we spent the day motoring.  

The forecast had a north west wind predicted to come in as we rounded the NW corner of Sicily, and I wanted to
catch it; if we were late, the NW wind would turn into a SE wind and we'd have a hard slog back to Tunisia.  As we passed 
Palermo we were still waiting on the wind shift and we could hardly do better than keeping to a course of 270.  Finally that wind came
around and we tacked into a course directly to Tunisia.  The wind was a bit higher than predicted and we sailed at 7-9 kts
all through the day and into the next morning with wind 15-19 kts just ahead of the beam.  Around 5 am, the captain was sound asleep when
we were hailed on the VHF, and a boat was rapidly overtaking us from the stern.  After rousing the captain, it turned out it was the Tunisian coast 
guard who wanted to know our destination, people on board, nationalities, etc.  They seemed satisfied with that and we didn't get boarded at least.

Next morning the wind was much less -- 3-5 kts close-hauled.  Finally a chance to use our code zero sail!  After some time spent getting it rigged, we were making 
60-70% wind speed which was good considering how weak the wind was.  On arriving in Yasmine Hammamet shortly before sunset, we moored at the customs dock and had the usual round 
of police, customs, and capitainerie agents to deal with.  We spent the night on the customs dock (with an outing to get dinner) and the next morning we
got diesel at the fuel dock (diesel is incredibly cheap in Tunisia) and then made our way to our assigned dock where New Horizon will be staying till next spring.
