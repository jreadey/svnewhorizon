After the passage from Malta to Sicily, from Oct 18 to Oct 30 we explored Sicily's east coast.  We first stayed a few days at the ancient (founded by the Greeks) city of Siracusa.  From there we had a 50 mile jump to the town of Taormina (featured in the White Lotus tv show).  After touring a Taromina and taxing on excursion to Mt Etna, we passed the Straits of Messina (the narrow body of water separating Sicily from mainland Italy) to arrive in Millazo, the
departure point for the Aeolian Islands (and an interesting city in its own right).

From Siracusa we motored 50 miles to the north to arrive in Taormina.  Taormina is a pretty city perched on the cliffs overlooking the Ionian sea.  Like Siracusa, it was founded by the Greeks and has many ancient ruins.  More recently, Taormina was the location of season 2 of "White Lotus".  Mt. Etna is a towering presence to the south and is usually spewing smoke (as it was when we visited).   We anchored with a few other boats just across from the train station (so heard the PA announcements day and night).  We were a bit south (and below) of Taormina proper, so we took a bus to get to the town and walk around.  Visiting the Greek ampitheater was even more walking uphill.  
There were even higher forts and/or little hamlets that we didn't have the time or energy to get to.  Going the other direction, we could walk south to
another ancient city of Nexos.

Mt. Etna is the highest peak in Sicily at about 11,000 ft and it's also one of Europe's most active volcanos.  It usually has several eruptions a year, but
was relatively quiet while we where there. It's a couple of hours away
by bus, but to save time we signed up for a tour that took us to the base of the Volcano, where we then took a gondola, and then a 4x4 to close to the 
top.  It was quite a different scene from the warm town below - much colder with howling wind.  We had a small hike at the upper level - it's soft volcanic sand without the smallest tree or blade of grass.

From Taormina, we continued north passing the staights of Messina (the narrow pass between Sicily and mainland Italy).  This is
where Ulysses encountered whirlpools and monsters, but having chosen a calm day to make the passage we saw no monsters and only
smaller whirlpools (the clashing waters of the Ionian sea to the south and the Tyrrhenian Sea create strong currents).  Or destination
was the city of Milazzo, the jumping off point for the Aeolian Islands to the north.  Despite the town being located next to an oil refinery,
it was quite nice.  After anchoring next to the many fishing boats in the harbour we were told by the officals that anchoring was strictly not allowed
and were told to get ourselves to a marina poste haste.  This wasn't all bad as it gave us a chance to fill our water tanks and clean the boat up a bit.
It was time for us to check out of EU, so we embarked on the challenging search of a police station that could electronically stamp our passports.  After
much discussion with different officals and a drive into town by a helpfl taxis driver we finally found the right office which was able to proess our passports after another hour of discussion and phone calls to the head office.

Anyway, back in Milazzo we walked up to the citadel that guards the entrance to the harbour and had a nice Italian dinner on the way back.
