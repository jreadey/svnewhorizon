After leaving Marseille, we had a short sail to the Iles de Hyres (Golden Islands), the 
start of the Côte d'Azur (or French Riviera).  We spent several weeks moving in short hops along the coastline. 
This region is a magnet for celebs and super-yachts, but Hyres
is a good introduction as it's very chill and laid back.  Hyres has several islands with our first stop being Porquerolles.
Biking is very popular on the island (there are many bike rentals), but we were happy to walk along and enjoy the ambience.
While we were at the anchorage, we had a surprise visit by the French Duane (customs).  They inspected our boat and had us fill
out some paper work, so at least the boat was admitted into the EU.  They are not the police though, so they couldn't process
our passports to properly admit us to the EU (we would attempt that in Nice).

Next we sailed back to the mainland to anchor off the Pampelonne Beach (near St. Tropez).  We landed the dinghy on beach
and then walked about a mile to the town of St. Tropez.  Did not see Brigitte Bardot, but it was a pleasant stop.

From St. Tropez we sailed to Cannes.  As luck would have it the Cannes Film festival was just getting started and 
the harbour was packed with super yachts.  Getting ashore was a bit of a challenge, but we managed to find a spot to beach the dinghy.
At Cannes tickets are only available to the well connected, but we were happy just to walk along the waterfront checking out
all the activity.

From Cannes, we anchored outside Antibes, a town that has a well-preserved medieval section.
Too bad the Picasso museum was closed for updating, but there was a museum of ancient Greek artifacts and a nice farmer's market.

Finally, we sailed to our final stop on the Côte d'Azur: Villefranche-sur-Mer, a small town just east of Nice.  VilleFranche was a perfect 
place to stay since the harbour had a nice anchorage, there was a dinghy dock on the town waterfront, and it was an easy bus (or train) 
ride to Nice (and other places we planned to visit, like Monaco).

In Nice we tried very hard to find an agency to formally admit us into the country, but no one would talk to us!  In the end we 
decided to just assume our stop by the French customs was our official entry into the EU.
