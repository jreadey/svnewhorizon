As we returned to Mallorca we were anticipating seeing the Solar Eclipse on Aug 12.  
The eclipse of 2026 after passing Iceland and mainland Spain basically ends at Mallorca,
the eclipse occurring just a few minutes before sunset.  Since the sight line to the 
eclipse was so low, being on a boat on the west side of Mallorca was actually perfect --
no buildings or hills to block the view.

As we headed out to a spot just south of Dragonera Island, we saw we were not the only
ones with this idea.  There were hundreds of other boats that came for the same view.
Arriving a few hours before sunset, we quietly drifted (it was too deep to anchor), waiting
for the eclipse.  Even though Mallorca in August is generally sunny, there were some low clouds and
I worried a bit that we might miss seeing the eclipse.

The partial phase of the eclipse lasted a couple of hours, but it was hardly noticeable unless you looked at the
sun with solar glasses.  It was only just before totality that there was a noticeable darkening.  And then suddenly 
totality happened and we saw a dark disk with the corona around it.  The sky wasn't perfectly clear, but we still had an excellent
view.  Meanwhile there was a lot of cheering and horn blowing from the neighboring boats.

Unfortunately, the eclipse was restively short, and after a minute the sun peeked out again.  By now it was about 10 minutes to sunset and we
had the very odd sight of a crescent sun sinking below the horizon.  We turned on the engine and navigation lights and headed east along the north 
coast of Mallorca.  This happened to be the second night of the Persiad Meteor shower and conditions with a new moon should have been excellent, but 
it was a bit of a bust with just a few meteor streaks now and then (though we did see one fireball).
