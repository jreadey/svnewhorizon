Finally it was time to leave Gibraltar.  We were headed to Valencia (about 400 sea miles from Gibraltar).  
Valencia had a North Sails loft and we planned on dropping off our badly damaged code zero to be repaired.
Things went well the first couple of days out of Gibraltar.  We stayed one night in an isolated harbour
on the Spanish coast and then pressed on towards Valencia.  As we were passing Cartagana, disaster struct!
We had just the Genoa out with a strong tail wind (~20 kts) pushing us along.  Early in the morning I looked out and - 
no jib!  The wind had ripped out the track that holds the sail to the jib stay and the sail was flying like
a pennant from the only remaining point of attachment at the top of the foil (the long metal cylinder that supports the luff 
of the sail).  

We turned on the engines and headed into the wind and tried to figure out how to secure the sail.  We obviously couldn't furler
it and it wasn't clear that releasing the halyard would lower the sail either as it was wrapped around the foil.  We eventually managed to 
wrap the sail around the foil  (not very elegantly), and use some lines to secure it until we could get to Valencia.

We thought it best to motor the rest of the way to Valencia, so we burned quite a bit of diesel when we could have been sailing.
In Valencia we were able to get the sail down and I went up the mast to examine it, but didn't see anything broken.  Looking at
the bottom of the foil we think what had happened is the shackle had come undown (bad zip tie?) which exposed the loft to the full force of the
wind which tore it out.

Anyway, in Valencia we had a proper rigger take a look who told us that the top of the foil was damaged and had to be fixed.  Luckily it didn't
need to be replaced totally and he was able to cut off the damaged part at the top.  At North Sails now we had two sails to be repaired.  The Genoa 
took only a few days and besides fixing the luff, the replaced the grommet and add some extra reinforcements to the tack, so better than new!
Meanwhile, the code zero needed many weeks of work, so we arranged to come back later in the summer to pick it up.
